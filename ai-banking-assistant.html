AI Banking Assistant

A voice-first banking assistant that authenticates a customer, understands what they're asking for in plain English, and answers with real account data, spoken back to them in under two seconds.

GPT-4o-mini Whisper TTS-1 Function calling SQLite Gradio

Most "AI in banking" demos stop at a chatbot that can define what a savings account is. I wanted something that could actually do the job: verify who you are, pull your real balance, search your transactions, and check whether you qualify for a loan, all through a conversation you could just as easily have out loud as type.

So that's what I built. The AI Banking Assistant is a full-stack application on top of OpenAI's GPT-4o-mini, wired up to a proper two-factor authentication flow, 8 specialized banking tools, and a voice pipeline (Whisper for speech-to-text, TTS-1 for the reply spoken back). It runs against a relational SQLite database seeded with 700 mock customers and over 1,000 transactions, so the numbers you see in the demo are real query results, not scripted responses.

Getting in the door: two-factor, done properly

Before the assistant tells you anything, it needs to know it's actually talking to you. Every session opens with a CX code, an 8-character identifier unique to each customer, checked against the database with a case-insensitive match. Get that right and it moves to a personal security question, and only after both checks pass does it create an isolated session tied to that customer's ID. Every single query after that is filtered by that ID at the database level, so there's no path for one customer's session to see another customer's data, even by accident.

Once you're in: the model decides what to do

This is where function calling earns its keep. GPT-4o-mini reads the request, figures out the intent, and picks from 8 registered tools: check a balance, list all accounts, pull recent transactions, search transactions by amount or date range, or assess loan eligibility against a credit score and income. The model extracts whatever parameters it needs straight from the sentence you typed or said, runs a parameterized query against the right table, and folds the result back into a natural reply. Ask "what's my checking balance" and it calls check_balance. Ask "show me everything over $100 last week" and it calls search_transactions with the amount and date window already filled in.

Making it talk

Text is the fallback, voice is the point. Whisper handles transcription on the way in, TTS-1 handles the spoken reply on the way out, and the whole round trip, speech in, model reasoning, database query, speech out, lands in under two seconds for a typical banking question. That's the part that made this feel less like a chatbot demo and more like a product: you can genuinely have a conversation with your account.

What actually broke, and what fixed it

A few things bit me along the way, and they were the useful parts. Early on, mock data generation created orphaned records across tables because foreign keys weren't enforced consistently, so I rebuilt the data generation to use real database IDs and added automated verification scripts to catch drift before it shipped. Authentication also failed more than it should have at first, security answers weren't matching despite correct input, which turned out to be a whitespace and casing issue, fixed with case-insensitive comparison and trimming plus better debug logging. And because this runs on a paid API, I added per-session rate limits and hard usage caps so a runaway loop can't turn into a runaway bill, keeping real-world cost to about $0.01 to $0.05 per conversation.
