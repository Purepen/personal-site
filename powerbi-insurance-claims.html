Insuring Success: a Car Insurance Claims Dashboard

Built for Onyx Data's monthly challenge: a Power BI dive into 38,000 car insurance policy holders, uncovering who actually files claims, which cars cost the most, and what the company should do about it.

Power BI DAX Onyx Data challenge

Onyx Data runs a monthly open challenge where they hand out a dataset and see what the community finds. This month it was car insurance: policy holder demographics, claim history, and the financial side of who actually gets paid out. I went in as an explorer with a dataset instead of a map, and came out with a full Power BI report and a set of recommendations I'd genuinely stand behind.

What the data actually said

  • Gender split among policy holders is essentially even, 49.9% male to 50.1% female, so gender isn't driving who insures a car.
  • Bachelor's degree holders make up exactly half the policy holder base, more than every other education level combined.
  • Only 44% of policy holders are parents, most are single, and average household income sits at $147,250.
  • Ford is the most insured make by a wide margin, and the Mustang specifically is both the most insured model and the one that gets paid out most.
  • The average age of a car behind a paid claim is 23 years, and private cars registered in urban zones account for roughly 83.71% more paid claims than other zones.

Turning insight into recommendations

A dashboard is only useful if it changes a decision, so the report closes with five concrete recommendations: run a proper risk assessment on urban zones where paid claims concentrate and price premiums accordingly, revisit coverage terms for the 23-year-old vehicle segment that drives disproportionate payouts, invest in loss prevention programs where claim frequency is highest, run driver education for policyholders to reduce avoidable claims, and tighten claims investigation to catch inflated or fraudulent payouts before they go out the door.
