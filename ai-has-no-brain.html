AI Has No Brain

It doesn't know anything, and it doesn't remember you. It's just extraordinarily good at guessing the next word, billions of examples deep.

Think about it like this. If I say "let me see you golololo", any Nigerian will instantly shout the next line, because you've heard it so many times your brain knows what comes next with near certainty. Same with "E don cast, last last." You already know what's coming. Or if someone says "the sky is", you'd probably say "blue," because that's what usually follows. That's literally how a language model works. Just with billions more examples, and a lot faster.

An LLM doesn't know anything in the way we mean when we say a person knows something. It isn't thinking, and it isn't conscious. It's really, really good at pattern matching and calculating probabilities from everything it's seen. At every step it's effectively asking itself: what word comes next, is it 78% likely to be "vibrant," 12% "warm," 10% "bright"? Then it picks one, usually the highest-probability option, and moves on. That's the whole trick behind what looks like magic.

Here's the part that made me laugh when I first understood it properly. Set the model's temperature to 0 and it becomes boring and predictable, always taking the top choice. Push temperature up toward 1.5 and it starts picking low-probability options on purpose, more creative, but a lot less coherent, like your most unpredictable friend at 2am. No hidden knowledge either way. Just patterns, and a dial that controls how much it's willing to gamble on the second-best guess.

The memory illusion is the wildest part once you see it clearly. An LLM doesn't actually remember your last message the way you'd remember a conversation from yesterday. Every single reply is generated by feeding the whole conversation history back into the model again, from scratch, so it can predict the next token with that context in front of it. That's what creates the feeling of continuity. Close the chat, and there's nothing left behind, it's the most extreme short-term memory you could imagine, reconstructed fresh every single time.

None of that makes it less useful. It's still a genuinely remarkable piece of engineering. But it's worth being precise about what it actually is: a calculator that got extremely good at guessing words, good enough that the guessing starts to look like understanding from the outside.
